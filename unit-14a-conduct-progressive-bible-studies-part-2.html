Opening Discussion · teach like Jesus

Heart of it: Jesus taught by example, by questions, and by illustrations. This unit focuses on those last two — the tools that reach the heart.

Jesus stimulated thinking with skillful questions and made truths unforgettable with simple illustrations. As we make disciples, exploring his methods helps us direct people to the congregation and build their love for Jehovah.

Proverbs 20:5 — "The thoughts of a man's heart are like deep waters, but the discerning man draws them out."

🔍Look it up: Luke 6:40·Mark 1:22

Jesus Used Questions · draw out the heart

Heart of it: Questions do more than check facts — they reveal what's in the heart and train the student to think.

QHow can "auxiliary" questions draw out a student's real thoughts and feelings?

Knowing something isn't the same as agreeing with it. Gentle follow-up questions — "Does this seem reasonable to you? How could you apply it?" — reach the heart. Be patient; don't rush the answer.

🔍Look it up: Matt. 16:13, 15·th study 3

QWhat if the student answers incorrectly — and how do we stay tactful?

Don't correct bluntly — ask another question to lead him to the right conclusion himself. "The tongue of the wise is a healing." And avoid questions that foster guilt; appeal to good motives instead.

🔍Look it up: Prov. 12:18·th study 19

Jesus Used Illustrations · pictures that stick

Heart of it: A good illustration simplifies, clarifies, and lodges truth in the memory — which is why Jesus used them constantly.

Matthew 13:34 — "Without an illustration he would not speak to them."

QWhat can well-chosen illustrations accomplish?

They arrest attention, stir thinking and emotion, aid memory, and overcome prejudice. For example, asking "What would compassion move you to do if you saw someone drowning?" gently motivates a hesitant student to share the truth.

🔍Look it up: Matt. 7:24-27·th study 8

QHow do we keep a study simple and moving at a good pace?

Focus on the main points; don't drag in unnecessary material. Give the student time to grasp and accept truths, cover only what he can absorb, and keep it progressing — "as you received Christ… go on walking in him."

🔍Look it up: John 16:12·Col. 2:6, 7

To the Congregation & Checking Progress · the goal is a disciple

Heart of it: The aim is baptism. That means drawing the student to the congregation and honestly checking that he's progressing.

QHow can you direct a student to the congregation?

Invite him from the very first study, describe the meetings, build anticipation for special events (the Memorial, assemblies), and mention the meetings when you pray. jw.org can help too.

🔍Look it up: lff \"Goal\" boxes

QWhy does helping a student progress take courage — even discontinuing a study?

Real love urges the student toward dedication and baptism, and lovingly addresses obstacles. If a study stays unproductive, we tactfully discontinue it — after prayer and a frank talk — so our time reaches hearts that respond, as "all… rightly disposed for everlasting life became believers."

🔍Look it up: Acts 13:48·Luke 8:15·w20.10 pp. 18-19
