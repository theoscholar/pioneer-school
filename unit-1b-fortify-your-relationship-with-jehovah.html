Opening Discussion · love that must be fed

Heart of it: Your friendship with Jehovah is the most valuable thing you have — and, like a living plant, it only stays strong if you keep feeding it.

King David said God's loyal love was "better than life" because closeness to the Giver of life is what makes living meaningful. We first loved God because he first loved us. Even Jesus never took his love for his Father for granted — he kept it strong his whole life. The three areas below are how we do the same: study & meditation, showing love, and prayer.

Psalm 63:3 — "Because your loyal love is better than life, my own lips will glorify you."

🔍Look it up: Ps. 63:3·1 John 4:19

Study and Meditation · feeding the mind and heart

Heart of it: Reading fills your head; meditating moves it to your heart. That's what turns facts into a real bond with God.

QHow does Isaiah's prophecy show the role of divine education between Jehovah and his Son?

Jesus is pictured as listening "morning by morning" (Isa. 50:4) like a taught one. Their closeness was built through teaching and learning — a pattern for us.

🔍Look it up: Isa. 50:4, 5

QWhat should be our goal in personal study?

Not just facts — a deeper love for Jehovah with our whole heart, soul, and mind. Study is a means to love, not an end in itself.

Matthew 22:37 — "You must love Jehovah your God with your whole heart and with your whole soul and with your whole mind."

🔍Look it up: Matt. 22:37

QWhat did Jehovah require of Israel's kings, and why? How do we cultivate the same desire?

Each king had to write out his own copy of the Law and read it daily, so he'd fear God and stay humble. We build that same appetite by longing for God's word like a newborn longs for milk.

🔍Look it up: Deut. 17:18-20·1 Pet. 2:2·w13 9/15 p. 30, par. 13

QWhy does daily Bible reading draw you closer to God? What is meditation, and on what should it focus?

Reading God's word "day and night" (Ps. 1:2) keeps him near — "Jehovah is with you as long as you remain with him" (2 Chron. 15:2). Meditation is thinking deeply and reflectively, focused on Jehovah's actions, qualities, and will.

Psalm 77:12 — "I will meditate on all your activity and ponder over your dealings."

🔍Look it up: Josh. 1:8·2 Chron. 15:2·1 Tim. 4:13-15·it-2 p. 363

QWhy is "coming to know" God more than academic study? How does Samuel illustrate it?

Knowing God is a relationship, not just information. Young Samuel "had not yet come to know Jehovah" (1 Sam. 3:7) until he personally heard and obeyed him — knowing grows through real experience with God.

🔍Look it up: John 17:3·1 Sam. 3:7-10

QWhat benefits come from digging into deep Bible truths — and why depend on "the faithful and discreet slave"?

Deep truths turn ordinary people into bold, mature ones (as the apostles showed). But like the Ethiopian official, we need a guide — Jehovah feeds us "food at the proper time" (Matt. 24:45) through the slave.

🔍Look it up: Acts 4:13·Heb. 5:12-14·Matt. 24:45·Acts 8:26-31

QHow did Jesus show appreciation for God's Word, and how does reflecting on creation help?

Jesus lived by "every word that comes from Jehovah's mouth" (Matt. 4:4). And creation itself puts God's invisible qualities on display — meditating on it presses spiritual truths into your heart.

🔍Look it up: Matt. 4:4·Rom. 1:20

Say it your way: Name one thing about Jehovah you'd love to meditate on this week — a quality, an act, or a part of creation.
Showing Our Love for Jehovah · love in action

Heart of it: Real love for God isn't only a warm feeling — it shows up in what we do.

QWhat is the connection between knowledge and love?

They grow together: accurate knowledge deepens love, and love makes us want to know God more.

🔍Look it up: Phil. 1:9, 10

QWhat shows our love for God is proven by actions, not just feelings?

The Bible ties love directly to obedience: "this is what the love of God means, that we observe his commandments."

1 John 5:3 — "For this is what the love of God means, that we observe his commandments; and yet his commandments are not burdensome."

🔍Look it up: 1 John 2:5·1 John 5:3

QHow do these scriptures show our love for God is genuine?

Each names a way love becomes visible — public declaration, clean conduct, speaking truth, a pure heart, meeting together, and practical help for those in need.

🔍Look it up: Rom. 10:10·2 Cor. 7:1·Eph. 4:15·1 Tim. 1:5·Heb. 10:23-25·1 John 3:17, 18

QHow can we show love in the way we use God's name, and keep our love "intense" (1 Pet. 4:8)?

By gladly making Jehovah's name known, as Jesus did. And by heeding Jesus' warning to the Ephesus congregation — never leave "the love you had at first" (Rev. 2:4).

🔍Look it up: John 17:6-8, 26·Rev. 2:1-5

Say it your way: Which of the "love in action" scriptures above describes something you already do — and one you'd like to grow in?
Draw Close Through Prayer · talking with your Father

Heart of it: Prayer is how friends stay close. The more freely you open your heart to Jehovah, the stronger the friendship grows.

QWhy should we view prayer as a precious privilege?

Jehovah is near to everyone who calls on him, and prayer brings a peace that "surpasses all understanding" (Phil. 4:7) to guard our hearts.

🔍Look it up: Ps. 145:18, 19·Phil. 4:6, 7

QHow important was prayer to Jesus, and how does regular prayer strengthen us?

In his hardest hour Jesus poured out his heart: "Abba, Father" (Mark 14:36). When we draw close to God in prayer, he draws close to us.

James 4:8 — "Draw close to God, and he will draw close to you."

🔍Look it up: Mark 14:36·Jas. 4:8

QWhat shows Jesus didn't rely on his own strength — and how can we pray with the same confidence?

Jesus leaned on his Father through every trial, and prayed with full trust that God heard him. We show the same faith, believing "he becomes the rewarder of those earnestly seeking him" (Heb. 11:6).

🔍Look it up: Matt. 4:7, 10·John 11:41, 42·Heb. 11:6
