Make Effective Return Visits

Opening Discussion · teaching, not just preaching

Heart of it: Many of us are in the truth because someone kept coming back. Return visits are where preaching becomes teaching — and they can be the most enjoyable part of the ministry.

Jesus said to make disciples, "teaching them." A teacher does more than proclaim — he explains and offers proofs. Three things make return visits effective: (1) sincere personal interest, (2) an appealing Bible subject, and (3) laying the basis for the next conversation.

Matthew 28:19, 20 — "Make disciples… teaching them to observe all the things I have commanded you."
Attitude & Groundwork · set up the next visit

Heart of it: A positive attitude and a little groundwork turn a first chat into an ongoing conversation. Return promptly — being "faithful in what is least" matters.

Luke 16:10 — "The person faithful in what is least is faithful also in much."

QWhat can we learn from Ananias' visit to Saul?

At first Ananias was hesitant — but he obeyed, and greeted Saul warmly as "Brother Saul," showing genuine care. The lesson: trust Jesus' direction, and go with real personal interest.

🔍Look it up: Acts 9:10-17

QHow do we lay the groundwork — and why leave a question unanswered?

Plant a question to answer next time: "How can we be sure Paradise will come in our lifetime? Let me stop back and show you." Building anticipation gives a natural reason to return.

🔍Look it up: th study 13·mwb16.01 p. 6

QWhy call back within a day or two, even if no literature was placed?

Interest fades fast, and research shows we care. Returning quickly — even just to drop off an article on the topic — keeps the interest warm. Literature placed or not, the person matters.

🔍Look it up: Matt. 10:13·be pp. 33-38

Your Objective & the Return Visit · aim for a study

Heart of it: The goal of every return visit is to start a Bible study. Small steps — answering a question, showing a video, considering a paragraph — lead there.

QHow do small goals move toward the main objective?

Each visit takes one step: answer a question with the Bible, show a relevant video, consider a paragraph from a study aid, then offer a study. Discussing a scripture every time makes the purpose clear.

🔍Look it up: lmd lesson 9

QWhat helps a return visit go well — and put the person at ease?

Remember you're a guest: be punctual, neatly dressed, appreciative, and respectful. Warm personal interest lessens apprehension; a short review of last time, plus honest enthusiasm, builds confidence in the Bible's message.

🔍Look it up: Phil. 1:27·w18.09 p. 32

Be Study-Conscious & Persevere · keep at it

Heart of it: Return visits take perseverance — but "God kept making it grow." Keep it simple, listen well, and don't give up.

1 Corinthians 3:6 — "I planted, Apollos watered, but God kept making it grow."

QWhy listen carefully — and why not condemn wrong views too soon?

Attentive listening honors the person and shows their thoughts matter. And before someone has grown to love spiritual things, correcting their views or habits can push them away — patience first.

🔍Look it up: w19.03 pp. 23-24

QWhat if the householder is busy, or even rude?

Leave a favorable impression regardless — share one brief scriptural thought with a busy person and set up the next call; stay kind if someone is abrupt. A prayerful attitude keeps us going.

🔍Look it up: Rom. 12:17, 18·w18.07 p. 28

Say it your way: Share one return-visit experience — what made it work? Get it ready as a comment.

Your quick prep plan ✅

  • 3 comments ready: the Ananias lesson, laying groundwork with a question, and aiming for a study.
  • 1 scripture to read aloud: Luke 16:10 or 1 Corinthians 3:6.
  • Practice: think of one "leave them wanting more" question you could plant this week.
