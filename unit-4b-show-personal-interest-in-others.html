Opening Discussion · a quality of the heart

Heart of it: Caring about people isn't a sales technique — it's real love that shows in how you listen, what you say, and even your face.

People everywhere respond to a warm smile, a listening ear, and sincere concern. Successful pioneers genuinely care and stay observant of others' needs. The four keys: encourage expression, really listen, ask good questions, and commend generously.

Philippians 2:4 — "Look out not only for your own interests, but also for the interests of others."

🔍Look it up: Gal. 6:10·Heb. 6:10·lmd lesson 1

Getting to Know People · read the person

Heart of it: Like Paul, we tailor our approach to the person in front of us — their background, interests, and circumstances.

QHow did Paul take his listeners' background into account?

In Athens he began with something they'd relate to — their "objects of veneration" (Acts 17:23) — and built a bridge from there. He met people where they were.

🔍Look it up: Acts 17:22, 23·Acts 26:2, 3

QWhat clues reveal a person's background — and how do current events help?

Look around: the home, yard, decorations, or religious items hint at their life and beliefs. A respectful comment on a current event can open a warm conversation — and acknowledging what they're busy with shows respect for their time.

🔍Look it up: w20.04 p. 10, pars. 8-10·John 4:7, 28, 29

QWhat if someone speaks another language — and how can jw.org help?

Be warm, and use jw.org to show content in their language; if they're interested, get help so they can be taught by someone who speaks it. Then follow up promptly.

🔍Look it up: od pp. 100-101·Rom. 12:14-16

Listen Attentively · the power of really hearing

Heart of it: Often people remember not what we said, but how well we listened. Listening honors them and opens hearts.

James 1:19 — "Everyone must be quick to listen, slow to speak, slow to anger."

QHow can questions show personal interest — without feeling like an interrogation?

Good questions invite people to share their opinion, showing we value their thinking. We keep it warm and gracious, not rapid-fire — a conversation, not a quiz.

🔍Look it up: Col. 4:6·th study 3·w14 9/15 p. 21, par. 16

QHow does listening honor others, and why make eye contact and commend?

Attentive listening says "your thoughts and feelings matter to me." Sincere eye contact and honest commendation make a person feel valued and at ease.

🔍Look it up: Rom. 12:10·be pp. 124-125

Be Adaptable · meet each person's need

Heart of it: Like Paul, we become "all things to people of all sorts" (1 Cor. 9:22) — adjusting our words to fit the young, the elderly, the grieving, the busy.

1 Corinthians 9:22 — "I have become all things to people of all sorts, so that I might by all possible means save some."

QHow might you show consideration for different people — and how far should you adapt?

Soften your approach for a grieving person, keep it short for someone ill or just woken, be upbeat with a youth. Adapt your manner and words, never the truth itself. Focusing on their needs also calms your nerves.

🔍Look it up: w20.04 p. 11, pars. 11-12

QWhen should you end a discussion, and why return on interest?

If a personal concern surfaces, center on what matters to them; and know when to close warmly. Returning promptly proves your interest was real — that's the deepest way we imitate Jehovah's love.

🔍Look it up: w19.11 p. 15, par. 8·Phil. 2:4

Say it your way: Think of one person in your territory — what's one adjustment that would show them you truly care?

Your quick prep plan ✅

  • 3 comments ready: reading the person, the power of listening, and adapting to each individual.
  • 1 scripture to read aloud: Philippians 2:4 or James 1:19.
  • Try it: in your next conversation, ask one extra question and really listen before replying.
